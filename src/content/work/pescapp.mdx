## The goal

Build an app that's easy to navigate and lets fishermen see where they are in real time, while the dock keeps a record of every trip. Out at sea there's often no coverage, so the app couldn't depend on a connection to track where a boat goes.

That meant delivering three things together:

- **An interface that's easy to use** on a phone, on a boat
- **A system for recording and storing** each boat's position
- **A platform for viewing routes** from the dock

## Tracking without coverage

The app works **offline first**:

- While a trip is active, the app records the boat's position every two minutes, along with speed, altitude, and accuracy. It runs as an Android foreground service, so tracking keeps going with the screen off.
- Every point is saved first to a local SQLite database on the phone, so nothing is lost when the boat is out of coverage.
- Every minute, a sync job checks for a connection and uploads any pending trips and points to Firestore, then cleans up old synced data.

## The dashboard

On land, a [Streamlit dashboard](https://github.com/ZaidDeAnda/pescapp-streamlit) reads the same Firestore collections. You pick a trip by date, and it draws the route on a Folium map, with a marker for every recorded point connected by a line.

## Working with the client

Before writing any code, we put together a proposal with the client that laid out the goal, the scope, the interface, and the architecture. Then we split the work into three sprints:

1. **Mobile app**: the Flutter app, with weather, the map, and trip tracking.
2. **Cloud and dashboard**: Firebase and the Streamlit dashboard.
3. **Testing and support**: stress tests, functional validation, and the start of support.
