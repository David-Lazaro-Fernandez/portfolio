## The idea

A personal project: rebuild the Xbox 360 "blade" dashboard as a website, and make its music player actually play, with visualizers in the spirit of the ones that came with the console. It's a fan recreation, not affiliated with Microsoft. [Try it live](https://david-lazaro-fernandez.github.io/Audio-Visualizer/blade-dashboard-mockups/) or read the [code on GitHub](https://github.com/David-Lazaro-Fernandez/Audio-Visualizer).

## The dashboard

It's a 10-foot UI, made to be read from a couch: large, high contrast, and driven by a D-pad cursor instead of a mouse. All five blades are built (Marketplace, Xbox LIVE, Games, Media, and System), and you can move through them with the arrow keys, the keyboard shortcuts for the controller buttons, or a real gamepad through the Gamepad API.

A few details that took the most care:

- **The blade curve.** No panel is a rectangle. Every edge, open or folded, comes from one "lazy S" curve: a straight drop, a near-vertical shoulder, a slight bow inward at the waist, and a flare at the bottom. It's sampled from two cubic Béziers so it's as smooth as an SVG path.
- **A water surface behind everything.** The background is a WebGL wave field, painted in each blade's color.
- **The small stuff.** The original navigation sounds, the sign-in toast, switching profiles, and screens like Achievements, My Games, and the picture browser.

## A music player that listens

The player plays real audio: each track is the store's 30-second preview, and a Web Audio `AnalyserNode` reads it as it plays, so every visualizer shows the spectrum of the actual song.

<Figure
  src="/work/audio-visualizer/music-player.png"
  width={1547}
  height={937}
  alt="Music Player with a fractal visualizer playing Digital Love by Daft Punk, next to the current playlist from Discovery"
  caption="The Music Player, with the playlist on the right."
/>

Getting it to look like the music took more than reading the FFT:

- **Logarithmic bands.** Splitting the FFT bins evenly puts everything from 20 Hz to 470 Hz in a single band, so the kick, the bass line, and most of the rhythm get one bar out of 28, and the display looks unrelated to the song. The bands are spaced about a third of an octave apart instead, which gives the bass ten bands.
- **Drops on the beat.** In the Water visualizer, every hit drops a ripple into the wave field. Drops fire on onsets, when a band jumps above its own recent average, not on loudness, which would fire 60 times a second. High bands also wait longer between drops, so hi-hats don't wash out the bass.

## Eleven visualizers

LED Matrix, Mirror Bars, Radial, Water, Spectrogram, Grid, Curl Field, Core, Warp, Harlequin, and Mandelbrot. The shoulder buttons cycle through them, as on the console. Warp is a port of the 1998 Geiss screensaver, and Harlequin runs on a small engine for MilkDrop-style presets, with per-frame and per-pixel equations warping the previous frame.
